Inside the Audit · Method & Deliverables

What a project actually looks like.

The exact steps, the systems we examine, the methodology, and what the client walks away with — written so both the board and the security team get value.

01 — The method

Five phases, four to six weeks.

A repeatable engagement. Same shape every time, so we can price it fixed and the client always knows where they are.

1

Agree the plan

We agree what we can test, when, and who to call. Nothing happens without your sign-off — and you can stop anything with one message.

Days 1–3
See a typical example+
Scope: “Test our public website, VPN and Microsoft 365. Leave the payments system alone. Test weekdays, 9–5. Main contact: Jane in IT.”
2

Map your systems

We find everything an attacker would see — what's open to the internet, who can log in, which suppliers connect to you, and where staff use AI tools.

Week 1
See a typical example+
What we find: two old servers nobody remembered, a login page with no two-factor, four staff passwords already for sale online, and three people pasting company data into ChatGPT.
3

Test your defences — safely

Senior experts and AI engineers try to break in the way a real attacker would, and chain weaknesses together. Live systems keep running; nothing is changed.

Weeks 2–4
See a typical example+
What happens: we get in through the out-of-date VPN, then use a leaked password to reach an admin account — showing how far a real attacker would get. No data is touched or damaged.
4

Sort what matters

We double-check every finding (no false alarms), rank them by real business risk, and tag each one to the law it affects.

Week 4–5
See a typical example+
Example: 23 issues found → 4 urgent (fix within 30 days), 9 medium, 10 low. Each tagged to the rule it touches (NIS2, DORA).
5

Report & walk-through

You get a short board report and a full technical pack, and we walk both teams through it live. We re-test once you've made the fixes.

Week 5–6
See a typical example+
You receive: a 1-page board summary (score: 58/100), a 20-page technical pack for the IT team, and a 45-minute call to walk both through it.
02 — What we examine

The whole estate an attacker would touch.

Not just the network. We look wherever an AI-equipped attacker would look first.

External attack surface

Websites, VPNs, email gateways, exposed servers and APIs — everything reachable from the internet.

Identity & access

Logins, multi-factor, admin accounts, joiners/leavers, and over-powered service accounts.

Email & people

AI-generated phishing resistance, awareness, and how staff actually behave under a realistic lure.

Cloud & infrastructure

Cloud configuration, storage exposure, secrets, and internal network segmentation.

Suppliers & third parties

The trusted vendors and integrations an attacker uses as a side door — a core DORA concern.

Your own AI & LLM use

Shadow AI, prompt-injection exposure, and data leaking into public AI tools — the newest gap.

03 — The methodology

AI-native testing, on recognised foundations.

Senior humans direct the engagement; AI does the heavy lifting at machine speed. We build on the standards security teams already trust — explained, never just name-dropped.

THE HUMAN + AI MODEL

Experts steer, AI scales

Senior cybersecurity experts set hypotheses and judge risk; AI engineers run tooling that finds and chains weaknesses far faster than a human could — the same shift attackers are exploiting.

SAFE BY DESIGN

No disruption, full audit trail

Testing is non-destructive and logged end to end. Rules of engagement are agreed up front; anything sensitive is confirmed before action.

Aligned to the frameworks your team knows:

OWASP web & API risks
MITRE ATT&CK attacker techniques
PTES testing standard
NIST controls
CREST-grade quality bar

We explain and align to these standards; specific certifications are pursued as the company matures.

04 — The deliverables

Built for two readers at once.

Every output speaks to the board and to the security team — the same truth, at two altitudes.

Key point

The board gets a decision. The security team gets a worklist. Both come from the same evidence.

Signal · the audit

What you receive from the audit

▰ For the board & execs (non-technical)
  • One Exposure Score (0–100) with a plain verdict
  • A one-page summary: are we at risk, and how badly
  • A plain-English "attack story" — how a breach would happen
  • The 90-day fix plan, costed and prioritised
  • Compliance status against NIS2 / DORA / CSA
▰ For the security team (technical)
  • Full findings register with severity, evidence & reproduction steps
  • Affected assets, CVE/technique references, proof-of-concept
  • Mapped to MITRE ATT&CK tactics
  • Concrete remediation per finding + retest criteria
  • Raw scan data and an exportable issue list
Shield · ongoing

What ongoing protection adds

▰ For the board & execs (non-technical)
  • A live exposure score that stays current — always audit-ready
  • Monthly one-page status; instant alert on anything serious
  • Evidence pack refreshed for regulators, on demand
  • One accountable provider across all your markets
▰ For the security team (technical)
  • Continuous / scheduled re-testing as the estate changes
  • Managed detection & response tuned for AI-speed attacks
  • Remediation tracking, retests, and a shared dashboard
  • New-threat sweeps when a relevant exploit appears
